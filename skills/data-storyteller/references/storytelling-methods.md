# Storytelling Methods — Application Guide

## Three-Act Narrative Arc

Structure data communication as: setup → conflict → resolution.

### Act I: The Beginning (Setup)
1. **Who must act, and what world are they in?** Frame the audience as the protagonist. Establish the relevant context (time frame, market, organizational situation).
2. **What is at stake?** Name the gap between "what is" and "what could be." Without a clear stake, the audience has no reason to stay engaged.

### Act II: The Middle (Conflict)
- **Cost of standing still:** use data to make the problem tangible — what deteriorates, what is lost, what risk grows without action.
- **Payoff of acting:** present the recommended solution, quantify benefits, connect directly to the gap from Act I. Explain why *this audience* is the right group to act.

### Act III: The End (Resolution)
- **Circle back:** remind of the problem and urgency from Act I.
- **State the call to action:** be explicit. "Approve the $200k reallocation by Friday" — not "let's discuss."

## Narrative Flow

- **Chronological:** walk through the analysis step by step. Use when building credibility with a new audience or when the process matters.
- **Lead with the Ending:** open with the recommendation, then provide evidence. Use when the audience is time-constrained or already trusts your work.

**Spoken vs. written:** live presentations keep slides sparse — your voice carries the connective tissue. Written reports and circulated decks state the takeaway explicitly on every page.

## Repetition Strategy

**"Bing, Bang, Bongo"** — reinforce at three points:
1. **Bing (Preview):** executive summary previewing what you'll cover and what you'll ask for.
2. **Bang (Detail):** full data, conflict, and evidence.
3. **Bongo (Recap):** restate key findings + repeat the call to action.

**Sound bites:** craft short, memorable phrases capturing the core message. If the audience can repeat your main point in their own words afterward, the message stuck.

## Logic Validation

### 1. Horizontal Logic
Extract every slide title (or section header) and read them in sequence as a paragraph.
- **Pass:** the paragraph tells a coherent, complete story.
- **Fail:** gaps or non-sequiturs → restructure the narrative.
- **Prerequisite:** only works with action titles (insight-driven), not topic labels.

### 2. Vertical Logic
Within any single slide or page: title states the insight, chart proves it, annotation explains it, nothing else is present. If an element doesn't support the title, move it to an appendix or remove it.

### 3. Reverse Storyboarding
After completing the deck, jot down the single takeaway from each page and compare against the original outline. Divergence means pages need to be added, removed, or reordered.

### 4. Fresh Perspective
Show the finished communication to someone unfamiliar with the topic and ask them to summarize the takeaways without your voiceover. Where they stumble is where the narrative needs tightening.

**If validation fails:** return to the narrative arc (Step 2 in the workflow) and restructure before attempting validation again.
