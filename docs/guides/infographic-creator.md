# Making an Infographic (infographic-creator)

**What it does:** combines several facts and numbers into one visual page that
tells a story people remember.

**Use it when:** you need a shareable one-pager — an annual-report summary, a
"year in review", a research recap for the public — where sticking in memory
matters as much as precision.

## A realistic use case

Your company's sustainability results are in: emissions down 40% since 2020,
12,000 trees planted, 3 new community programs, 98% employee participation.
You want a one-page visual for the website and LinkedIn.

## Try it now (copy-paste and edit)

```
/dataink:infographic-creator Make a one-page infographic from our annual
impact report: emissions down 40% since 2020, 12,000 trees planted, 3 new
community programs, 98% employee participation. Audience: customers and the
public. Goal: they should see us as serious about sustainability.
```

## What happens next

1. Claude starts with the story, not the pictures: what's the beginning,
   middle, and end of this page? Which fact is the headline and which are
   support?
2. Big single numbers get displayed as exactly that — a big number with a
   short phrase. Only real comparisons and trends become mini-charts.
3. Each panel gets one insight and one focal point, arranged so your eye
   walks through the page in story order.
4. Because this is public-facing, a little decoration is allowed — icons, a
   distinctive look — but the *data* itself is never bent: bar lengths and
   positions stay honest even when the styling is playful.
5. Colors get the same treatment as everywhere else in DataInk: checked with
   a tool for readability and color-blind safety, one highlight color, calm
   everything else. Each panel gets a text alternative for screen readers.

## How to know you got a good one

- Someone scrolling past can absorb the headline in a couple of seconds.
- The page reads in order — you always know where to look next.
- A week later, people can still quote the big number. That's the whole point.

## Things it will push back on (and why)

- **"Make it pop with 3D and lots of graphics"** — 3D bends the numbers, and
  when everything pops, nothing does. You'll get bold typography, one strong
  color, and clean alignment instead — which is what actually looks premium.
- **A wall of disconnected facts** — ten facts with no order isn't an
  infographic, it's a collage. It will make you pick a headline.
